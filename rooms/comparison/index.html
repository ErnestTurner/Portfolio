TRIKZIK LABS / SPATIAL STUDY 01

A room, then an instrument.

A bounded study of the entrance hall, the workbench and Scribble Engine. The other ten rooms retain their first version. Warm plaster, painted metal, oak and daylight replace the flat panels; the humor remains in what happens when you use things.

The two rooms now use real 3D geometry, perspective cameras, depth testing and cast shadows. Choose a wall to change the view. On a small screen, tap a doorway to identify it, then tap again to enter. The room index opens any destination directly.

The workbench moves closer to the instrument before opening its working surface. Step back pauses and retains that session. Reloading starts a new instrument session; export artwork and download setups before leaving. The drawing sheet and museum copy retain their separate existing preview storage.

What this study establishes

These are authored, fixed-camera rooms. They are intentionally simple mesh models, not photorealistic environments or free-roaming game levels. The instrument keeps its original two-dimensional drawing engine, workspaces, recipes, replay and exports. The object on the distant bench is a static model; the working instrument opens when selected.

Keyboard navigation, a direct directory, motion pause and reduced-motion preferences remain available. If WebGL is unavailable, the scene has an explicitly labeled illustrated fallback. The renderer draws on entry, resize and brief camera moves; it does not continuously redraw an idle room.

Comparison baseline: preview commit 633140b. No production site, domain, billing, permission, existing save format or other toy was changed.
